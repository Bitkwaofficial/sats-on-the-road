02b / Why we fly now

What Pilot Phase 1.0 taught us.

Pilot Phase 1.0 (April 2026) drove 3,000+ km through three countries, Benin, Togo and Burkina Faso, from our Nigeria base. It onboarded 1,000+ people and proved the idea works. It also showed us, the hard way, what to change for the other eight.

  • Borders ate days and money

    Seme, Hilacondji and Cinkansé each cost time and cash. Overland crossings were the single biggest drain on the trip.

  • Fuel ran out

    Long stretches of the Togo to Burkina road had no fuel, or only jerrycans at the roadside.

  • The car broke down

    A shock absorber failed with no budget to fix it, on roads one car was never built for.

  • One person could not do it all

    You cannot teach, onboard and film at the same time. The pilot taught thousands but filmed almost nothing.

  • It came out of one pocket

    The whole pilot cost about USD 3,400, mostly personal, with USD 1,000 from Conférence Bitcoin Afrique.

The new approach

Fly in. Drive local. Film everything.

We stop driving across borders. In each country we fly in, then rent and brand a local vehicle, a Toyota Sienna, with a local driver who knows the roads and the fuel. We work the markets, schools and villages for about twelve days, hand the work to local ambassadors, then fly to the next country. A two-person team travels now: one leads teaching and onboarding, one documents every stop. Flights cost less than the hidden price of borders, fuel gaps and breakdowns, and the trip is safer, faster, and captured on camera from start to finish.
